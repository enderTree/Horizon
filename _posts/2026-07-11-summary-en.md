## [EU Says Meta's Addictive Design Violates DSA, Faces $12B Fine](https://www.theverge.com/policy/963872/meta-eu-addictive-design-200b-fine-risk-digital-services-act-dsa) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The European Commission has preliminarily ruled that Meta's Facebook and Instagram employ addictive design features—such as infinite scroll, autoplay, and personalized recommendations—that violate the Digital Services Act (DSA). Meta now faces a potential fine of up to $12 billion (6% of global annual revenue) and may be required to redesign its platforms. This marks one of the first major enforcement actions under the DSA targeting addictive design patterns, potentially setting a precedent for stricter regulation of social media platforms globally. If upheld, it could force Meta and other companies to reduce engagement-maximizing features that harm user mental health. The EU specifically criticized Meta's limited time-management tools as ineffective and demanded default-off settings for certain features, effective screen breaks, and reduced engagement-driven algorithms. The preliminary ruling is not final, and Meta can respond before a decision is made.

telegram · zaihuapd · Jul 10, 14:47

**Background**: The Digital Services Act (DSA) is an EU regulation that came into force in 2022, establishing comprehensive rules for digital services, including obligations for very large online platforms (VLOPs) to assess and mitigate systemic risks like addictive design. Addictive design patterns, such as infinite scroll and personalized algorithms, exploit psychological vulnerabilities to maximize user engagement, raising concerns about mental health impacts, especially among younger users.
