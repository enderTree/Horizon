## [Mojo programming language open-sourced under Apache 2.0](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/18/mojo-is-now-open-source/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Mojo's compiler and toolchain are now open source under the Apache 2.0 license, following the release of Mojo 1.0. This fulfills a commitment made in May 2023. Mojo is a high-performance language designed for AI and GPU programming, so open-sourcing it could accelerate adoption and community contributions. This marks a significant milestone for the AI programming ecosystem. Mojo originally aimed to be a superset of Python, but that goal was deprioritized around August 2025. Mojo is now optimized for GPU programming with Python-like syntax, and is built on the MLIR compiler framework.

rss · Simon Willison · Aug 18, 21:39

**Background**: Mojo is a systems programming language for Linux and macOS developed by Modular Inc., designed for AI infrastructure and heterogeneous hardware. It builds on the MLIR compiler framework rather than directly on LLVM, enabling higher-level compiler optimizations and support for CPUs, GPUs, TPUs, and other accelerators. This allows developers to write high-performance code for AI applications more easily.

## [Repairing a Bricked Framework Laptop with $20 Tools Sparks Liability Debate](https://quantum5.ca/2026/08/16/fixing-bricked-amd-7040-series-framework-13-laptop-with-20-tools/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A user published a detailed blog post on August 16, 2026, showing how they revived a Framework Laptop 13 with an AMD 7040-series CPU that had been bricked by a BIOS update, using only about $20 worth of tools. The repair required pogo pins because Framework does not populate the BIOS flashing header on the motherboard. The story highlights a common but under-discussed risk: BIOS updates can permanently disable a functioning laptop, and most users lack an easy recovery path. It also puts pressure on manufacturers like Framework to take more responsibility for firmware failures and provide accessible recovery options, which is central to the repairability and sustainability movement. The seemingly dead laptop was an AMD 7040-series Framework Laptop 13, and the fix cost about $20 in tools. Framework leaves the official JSPI debug/flashing connector unpopulated to save cost, which forced the author to use pogo pins to contact the pads and reflash the BIOS chip.

hackernews · jp_sc · Aug 18, 13:18 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49345220)

**Background**: A "bricked" device is electronics that no longer works, often because a firmware update failed or was interrupted, leaving it as useful as a brick. Framework laptops are known for modular, user-repairable designs, but this incident shows that firmware-level failures still present a serious challenge. Robust firmware update systems normally validate and roll back to a last known good version, but when that fails, hardware flashing tools may be required to recover the device.

An essay examines the impossible position corporate employees face when state coercion conflicts with company values, arguing that legal and moral obligations can diverge sharply. Commenters debate trust, technology, and the limits of corporate power in civil society. This discussion matters because it exposes the fragility of civil society when corporations are forced to choose between loyalty to shareholders and obedience to authoritarian rulers. It also highlights how everyday technologies like WiFi, cameras, and LLMs can become tools of state control. Commenters point out that legal loyalty must go to the country's laws, but moral loyalty belongs to the Universal Declaration of Human Rights. Others note that technology cannot solve social problems—societies solve them using technology, and trust is the essential glue that makes civil society work.

**Background**: Civil society depends on a collective assumption that most people will do the right thing, and it actively works to isolate those who betray that trust. When a government has guns, corporate employees may face a direct conflict between obeying the state and respecting their parent company's ethical standards. This tension becomes especially acute for multinational corporations operating in countries with authoritarian regimes, where the rule of law and human rights are not aligned.

## [TrendForce: Chinese AI Chips to Take Nearly 90% of Domestic Market by 2026](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/chinas-homegrown-ai-accelerators-to-supply-90-percent-of-the-countrys-domestic-market-analysts-suggest-cambricon-and-huawei-expected-to-be-the-biggest-winners-in-the-shift-away-from-nvidia-and-amd) ⭐️ 8.0/10

TrendForce projects that Chinese domestic AI accelerators will supply nearly 90% of China's market by 2026, up from about 45% last year. In 2025, Nvidia shipped 2.2 million units for a 55% share, while Huawei shipped 812,000 units for a 20.3% share, with Cambricon and Huawei expected to be the biggest beneficiaries. This shift signals a major realignment of the AI hardware market away from Nvidia and AMD amid US export controls, making China's domestic supply chain increasingly self-sufficient. It could reshape global semiconductor competition and affect AI development costs and chip availability in China. To hit TrendForce's target, China must raise high-end AI chip production by 2.2 times to roughly 1.96 million units within a year, raising questions about whether manufacturing capacity can keep up. The forecast covers AI accelerators such as Cambricon and Huawei Ascend products, not general-purpose GPUs.

telegram · zaihuapd · Aug 18, 13:03

**Background**: An AI accelerator, also known as an AI chip or neural processing unit, is specialized hardware designed to speed up AI and machine learning workloads such as neural networks. Cambricon Technologies, founded in 2016 by researchers from the Chinese Academy of Sciences, develops AI chips and has emerged as a potential domestic rival to Nvidia. Huawei's Ascend product line, including the Ascend 310 and Ascend 910 chips, was announced in 2018 and underpins Huawei's cloud AI services. US export controls on advanced chips have accelerated China's push to replace imported accelerators with domestic alternatives.
