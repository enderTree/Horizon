## [Google Builds a $200B Wall Street Financing Machine for Anthropic AI Chips](https://www.ft.com/content/549f2e23-5aa2-49c7-9ea6-a9784ab7087c) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The Financial Times reports that Google has quietly assembled one of the largest infrastructure financing structures ever, with contracts totaling about $200 billion to deliver over $150 billion of AI chips to Anthropic. In June, a special purpose vehicle called Compute SPV completed its first deals, buying roughly $35 billion in hardware, about 1 gigawatt of compute and 1 million TPUs. This is a paradigm shift in AI infrastructure financing, letting a company without a credit rating like Anthropic access enormous compute while spreading risk across Google, Broadcom, Apollo, Blackstone and Morgan Stanley. The structure is similar to aircraft leasing and could become a template for future mega-scale AI projects. Google guarantees data centers, Broadcom buys and helps finance the chips, while Apollo and Blackstone purchase hardware and lease it back to Anthropic. About 80% of the contracts are tied directly to chips, and the involvement of crypto miners reflects the unusual risk-sharing in the deal.

**Background**: A special purpose vehicle (SPV) is a separate legal entity created to hold assets, manage risk, or raise money for a specific project, often to isolate financial risk. Google's Tensor Processing Unit (TPU) is a custom-designed ASIC accelerator optimized for machine learning workloads. The financing model borrows from vendor-financing practices used by Boeing and GE to sell aircraft, allowing parties to avoid putting hundreds of billions in AI hardware on their balance sheets.

## [China's First Mandatory L3/L4 Autonomous Driving Safety Standard Drafted](https://t.me/zaihuapd/42972) ⭐️ 9.0/10

China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) has completed the draft of the mandatory national standard 'Safety Requirements for Automated Driving Systems of Intelligent Connected Vehicles'. The draft is open for public comment starting June 17 and is recommended to take effect on July 1, 2027. This is China's first mandatory national standard for L3 and L4 autonomous driving, marking a shift from flexible marketing claims to enforceable safety obligations. It will significantly affect automakers and the broader autonomous vehicle industry by requiring systematic, evidence-based safety validation before deployment. The standard introduces a Safety Case mechanism that requires companies to systematically demonstrate safety using the 'claim-argument-evidence' structure. It also sets distinct requirements for L3 human-machine handover scenarios and L4 autonomous handling of system risks.

telegram · zaihuapd · Aug 4, 13:06

**Background**: L3 and L4 are levels of automated driving defined by SAE International. L3 (conditional automation) allows the vehicle to drive itself under certain conditions but requires the human driver to take over when requested, while L4 (high automation) enables the vehicle to handle all driving tasks within its operational design domain without human fallback. A Safety Case is a structured, evidence-based argument that a system is acceptably safe for deployment, typically organized as claims, arguments, and evidence. Making this a mandatory national standard in China legally obligates automakers to provide such proof, rather than relying on marketing language.

Shai-Hulud, named after the sandworm in Dune, is a worm-like malware family that self-propagates through npm packages. JFrog's security research team detected this new wave and published a detailed analysis.

## [FedEx's Legitimate Emails Train Users to Fall for Phishing, Troy Hunt Shows](https://www.troyhunt.com/thanks-fedex-this-is-why-we-keep-getting-phished/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Security researcher Troy Hunt published a post demonstrating how FedEx's legitimate email notifications closely mimic the patterns of phishing emails. The post argues that such official corporate communications are actively conditioning recipients to ignore the warning signs that security awareness training teaches. When trusted companies send emails that look like phishing, they undermine security awareness campaigns and make real attacks more likely to succeed. This affects every FedEx customer and the broader cybersecurity landscape, highlighting that email authentication alone cannot fix confusing sender and link practices. Examples cited in the discussion include a legitimate FedEx customs notice sent as a plain email from an individual employee with a PDF attachment, and the Australia branch being named 'FedEx Express' after the TNT acquisition, i.e., 'Federal Express Express'. Commenters also note that even shorter Google links such as 'c.gle' look suspicious to ordinary users, and that random new gTLDs make phishing harder to spot.

hackernews · stymaar · Aug 4, 21:09 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49175192)

**Background**: Phishing is a social engineering attack in which fraudsters send deceptive emails to trick recipients into clicking malicious links, opening infected attachments, or revealing credentials. Common defenses include security awareness training, phishing simulations, and email authentication protocols such as SPF, DKIM, and DMARC, which help receiving servers verify that a message genuinely comes from the claimed domain. However, these technical measures do not address bad user experience, confusing brand naming, or legitimate senders whose emails still resemble common phishing indicators.

The OpenAI Responses API, launched in March 2025, unifies chat completion and tool-calling capabilities for agentic applications. Content-addressable logging stores log entries by their content hash, enabling deduplication and more efficient storage.
