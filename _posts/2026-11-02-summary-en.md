## [Terry Tao: Mathematics Has Changed, and Credit Norms Must Adapt](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/01/mathematics-has-changed-lets-act-like-it/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

In a November 1, 2026 blog post, Terence Tao argues that AI has fundamentally changed mathematical practice and that the field should revise its norms—especially the criteria for a substantial contribution—to recognize work such as choosing a worthwhile problem, obtaining a proof entirely using AI, understanding it thoroughly, and developing further questions. Tao is widely regarded as one of the world's leading mathematicians, so his call to rethink credit allocation could affect how hiring, promotion, publication, and funding evaluate AI-assisted work; it also signals that the scarce skills in research may shift from producing proofs toward problem selection, verification, and explanation. The proposal is a normative argument about credit rather than a claim that current AI systems can independently conduct research; Tao's example grants substantial credit even to someone who supplies no new ingredient in the original proof, while commenters note that AI-generated proofs can be long, ugly, and hard to understand before later simplification.

hackernews · smilelamp · Nov 1, 18:28 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50328251)

**Background**: Terence Tao is a widely cited mathematician whose blog is closely followed by researchers; AI-assisted mathematical workflows now integrate AI into the research lifecycle, from exploration and proof search to verification and literature review, rather than only automated theorem proving. Mathematics has long relied on social norms—peer review, attribution, and reputation—to decide which contributions matter, and those norms are being tested as AI tools make certain parts of proof production cheaper.

**Discussion**: Hacker News commenters largely agreed that 'digestion'—understanding, contextualizing, and extending AI-produced results—will become a core mathematical skill, while some framed the shift as mathematics moving from craft production to industrial production. Others pushed back on the assumption that AI capabilities will stop at theorem proving, and several noted that many mathematicians feel discouraged by long, inelegant machine-generated proofs and by the sudden change in the field's social foundations.
