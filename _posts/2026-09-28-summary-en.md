## [Simon Willison's Annotated Keynote: 2026 in LLMs So Far](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/27/2026-in-llms-so-far/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Simon Willison published an annotated version of his closing keynote at the WeAreDevelopers World Congress North America, delivered in San Jose on 25th September 2026, walking chronologically through the major LLM developments of the year so far. The accompanying notes and slides trace the story back to what he calls the November 2025 inflection point, when Claude Opus 4.5 and GPT-5.1 were released. Willison is one of the most widely read independent commentators on LLMs, so his chronological synthesis offers developers a compact, opinionated map of a year that has otherwise been scattered across dozens of separate model launches and blog posts. For teams deciding which models and coding agents to adopt, this kind of retrospective is often more useful than any single product announcement. Willison argues that the November 2025 releases of Claude Opus 4.5 and GPT-5.1 were individually incremental, but together crossed an invisible line that turned coding agents from "often make mistakes" into "reliable enough to use on a day-to-day basis" when paired with their harnesses. He also cites his long-running and deliberately silly benchmark — asking models to generate an SVG of a pelican riding a bicycle — noting that as of November neither model could really draw a working bicycle.

## [China's Delivered Data Center Capacity Tops 24GW, Surpassing EMEA and APAC Combined](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/the-chinese-ai-infrastructure-boom) ⭐️ 8.0/10

SemiAnalysis's latest model estimates that China's delivered data center capacity has exceeded 24GW across more than 60 operators and 1,000-plus facilities, overtaking EMEA and the rest of Asia-Pacific combined. ByteDance alone accounts for roughly 20% of national delivered capacity and set a record of landing 100MW within 12 months at a core node, while Alibaba, Tencent and Baidu saw combined capex surge to about $20 billion in 2026Q2 (roughly double year-on-year), marking the first time all three simultaneously posted negative free cash flow. The finding is a major correction to a metric the market has long underestimated, showing China now hosts the world's second-largest physical compute pool after North America and reshaping assumptions about global AI capacity distribution. It also signals that China's AI infrastructure race has entered a heavy-asset phase where hyperscalers are betting directly on power and land, with cash-flow pressure that could affect future capex pacing and the broader supply chain. Much of the 24GW comes from existing retail colocation being quickly "retrofitted" into AI clusters via high-density electrical systems and liquid cooling upgrades, rather than purely greenfield construction. The figures come from SemiAnalysis's own model rather than official statistics, and GW here measures power/electrical capacity rather than actual delivered AI compute, so it should be read as an upper-bound estimate of the physical foundation.

**Background**: SemiAnalysis is an independent research firm covering the semiconductor and AI supply chain end to end, known for unconventional data collection such as public-records requests, shipping-manifest analysis and drone flyovers of data center sites to obtain information that is technically public but not widely circulated. In data centers, capacity is usually measured in megawatts or gigawatts because power supply — not floor space — is the binding constraint for AI clusters; high-density racks now exceed 100–200kW and require liquid cooling because traditional air cooling cannot remove heat efficiently enough. Free cash flow is the cash left after operating expenses and capital expenditures, so a negative figure means a company is spending more on building capacity than its operations generate, which is a defining feature of an infrastructure arms race.
